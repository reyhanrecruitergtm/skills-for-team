## Matt's LinkedIn strategy notes (verbatim)

> On LinkedIn, copy is king.
> Use your copywriting chops to create remarkable long-form content.
> Long-form lists, carousels, and images work great.
> Always offer value. Craft tight hooks. And keep them reading.
> Pro tip: LinkedIn doesn't like you sharing links to external sites.
> To get around this, wait 45 minutes or more for your post to get traction.
> After it gets some visibility, edit the link into the post after the fact.

## 1. "10 steps to protect your focus (my deep work routine)"

- **URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7163518047738126337/ · 1,893 comments
- **Format (his label):** Long-Form List + Long-form Image.
- **Why It Works (his breakdown, verbatim):**
> **A Strong Hook** — On LinkedIn, you only get an image + 3 lines of text to grab attention. This is called "the Hook." Everything else is hidden after a "See more" clickable link. To super power your hook, I always recommend using specific numbers ("10 steps to protect your focus") and specific promises (my deep work routine) that resonate with your audience. This hook also shows the first of the 10 tips ("1. Eliminate Distractions") without explaining all the details. If you're intrigued, you have to click to get more.
> **The Image as Hook** — This post has a "Long-form Image." This image is a handwritten page from my notebook and full of even more value. The image and the text from the post combine to keep people reading my posts much longer than the average LinkedIn content.
> **Lists Keep People Scrolling** — Lists get lots of interest on LinkedIn and keep people reading longer, which LinkedIn loves (and rewards). Keep the lines short and sweet so that people keep scrolling. Pack each line with value based on your ideal customer.

## 2. "How to design your dream life in 2024."

- **URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7167506788794236928/ · 1,541 comments
- **Format:** Hook Stacking + Long-form Image.
- **Why It Works (verbatim):**
> This post is both evergreen and timely. It's evergreen in the sense that it will never grow old. Everyone wants to design their dream life. But it's timely in the sense that in the first quarter of the new year, people are ready to be inspired and make changes in their life. This is a content format and hook that could be attached to many different posts. It's very strong.
> Notice the "I" voice. Using the I voice is very strong because it grounds a post in your personal story and perspective. I also share quotes, exercises, advice, and anecdotes. This keeps the post from being stale. It just flows.
> The post has many short, individual lines. You want to make each line of your post short, sweet, and memorable. Keep people reading. Keep them interested. Earn their attention line by line.

## 3. "Procrastination is the worst disease of the 21st century."

- **URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7165694165404561409/ · 1,370 comments
- **Format:** Long-form List + Long-form Image.
- **Why It Works (verbatim):**
> **Find a Winning Structure.** There's a simple, stanza-esque rhythm to this post that I repeat over and over. It's a list. But each item is formatted like this:
> **Do This:** Each list item is a short command.
> **Clarify:** The list item is written to be succinct, so the second line brings clarity (or extends the idea).
> **3x Tasks:** Here are the practical action items to give each list item meat and definition.
> **Bring it Home:** Underline the main idea in a fresh way. High energy to transition to the next point.

## 4. "How to build killer SEO."

- **URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7164244329941692416/ · 565 comments
- **Format (his labels):** Mini-Course · Nested Lists · Combine Niches.
- **Why It Works (verbatim, condensed):**
> This is basically a mini-intensive course on SEO. Sometimes it can be helpful to distill a large topic in a simplified, bite-size manner. You could get multiple videos or blog posts of value out of this one post alone.
> I use lists within lists, and single-sentence lines to keep things fresh and simple. I like nesting bullets or numbered lists within the main numbered list.
> I'm not an SEO influencer, but getting attention on your content is important to my audience. So I've shared my distillation of a technical topic to a non-technical audience. Try to find areas just outside of your audience's core competence, then interpret those areas for them.

## 5. "I'm obsessed with great leadership."

- **URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7179086460136902657/ · 557 comments
- **Format (his labels):** FOMO · Rhythm · Sharing an Ideal.
- **Why It Works (verbatim, condensed):**
> This hook combines two powerful things: "I" Language (personal language is stronger than general abstractions) and Fear of Loss (I wasted years of opportunities. The question is — will you too?). It's hard to resist spending a few minutes reading something that could save you from years of pain.
> You never want to repeat yourself in your content. But it's great to repeat the beginnings of your lines. This repetition at the start of each line creates a sense of rhythm that keeps you reading. This is similar to how songs are structured as verses, bridges, and choruses. The familiarity is comforting.
> If you can articulate an ideal, it's powerful and inspiring.

- **URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7016750171849900032/ · 375 comments

## 10. "I can teach you more in 2 minutes than you'll learn in 4 years."

- **URL:** https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7171854430617726976/ · 320 comments
- **Format (his label):** Balance the Specific and General.
- **Why It Works (verbatim, condensed):**
> A lot of good posts fall in two buckets. Too general. Or too specific. A pure list of raw tactics and recommendations can be too dry. And a general list of high level strategy can be too heady and vague. Often, the best posts switch between these modes. Think of a ping pong ball bouncing between two sides. High level. Specific. High level. Specific. You never zone out, because the variation keeps you engaged. Combine that with a great image distilling the points? You've got a winner.

## The mechanics worth stealing for RecruiterGTM content (our note, not Matt's)

1. Specific number + specific promise in every hook, with tip #1 visible before the fold.
2. The long-form image as a second hook — handwritten/notebook aesthetic beats polished design.
3. The 4-beat list rhythm: command → clarify → tasks → bring it home.
4. Interview distillation (his Leila post) — recruiters sit in interviews all day; every strong hiring-manager or candidate conversation is a Star Power Interview post waiting to happen.
5. Ping-pong between high-level principle and specific tactic to hold attention.
6. Repeat line-beginnings for rhythm, never content.
