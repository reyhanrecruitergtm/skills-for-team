# Annotated Examples — slop → human, with the reasoning

The teaching file for the team. Templates produce slop; understanding *why* a line works produces copy. Read this before writing any cold DM / email / follow-up, alongside `swipe-file.md`.

**How to use it:** don't copy the "good" lines — copy the *thinking* in the WHY. The situation changes every time; the reasoning transfers. Every pair maps to a rule in `SKILL.md` (Section B rubric + Section D craft). The "❌ bad" lines are illustrative team-slop, not real client copy. The "✅ good" lines are drawn from our real locked plays (`rgtm-outbound/copy-framework.md`) and Reyhan's proven DMs.

> The one habit that fixes most of this: **answer the 3 pre-writing questions (D1) before you draft.** What's the ONE situation? What's their "hell no"? What's the single most valuable give? If you can't answer them, you're not ready to write — and the copy will come out generic.

---

## 1. The opener — throat-clear vs situation

**❌ Slop**
> Hi {{first}}, I hope this email finds you well. My name is Reyhan and I'm the founder of RecruiterGTM. I wanted to reach out because I came across your profile and was really impressed with what you're building in the recruitment space.

**Why it's slop:** four lines before a single idea. "Hope this finds you well" / "wanted to reach out" / "came across your profile" are the three most-deleted openers in cold email. It's all about *us* (name, company, our impression), nothing about *them*. The reader has decided it's a pitch before line 2.

**✅ Human**
> Hey {{first}},
>
> Found you while looking for recruitment leaders in {{niche}}.
>
> Most of a {{niche}} recruiter's week goes on admin that never needed a human — sourcing, list-building, the first draft of every outreach.

**Why it works:** line 1 is honest and specific (why you, why now). Line 2 drops them straight into *their* week, not our product. It passes the 4-second skim test — they know what this is about and whether it's them. **Rules:** B1 (first line earns the open), B4 (personal not promotional), D2 (situation over claims).

## 2. Feature-dump vs the gap they live with — the #1 slop fix (D2)

**❌ Slop**
> We've built an AI-powered, end-to-end recruitment automation platform that leverages Claude to streamline your entire sourcing workflow, optimise candidate engagement, and unlock unprecedented efficiency across your desk.

**Why it's slop:** this is our brochure read aloud. "Leverages," "streamline," "optimise," "unlock unprecedented efficiency" — trigger words stacked three deep (anti-slop Layer 2). It lists what the product *does*. A recruiter can't picture themselves anywhere in it. Features are our vocabulary; the reader only recognises *situations*.

**✅ Human**
> You drop in a role and it sources candidates from your ATS and the web, then drafts the outreach for each using your defined framework so it's not AI slop.

**Why it works:** same product, but it's a *scene they can picture* — you drop in a role, it does the grind, you get drafts back. Concrete verbs (drop in, sources, drafts), zero adjectives selling it. "so it's not AI slop" pre-empts the obvious objection in five words (D5). **Rules:** D2, D3 (homework test), B11 (show don't tell), anti-slop L2.

## 3. Jargon vs the party test (D3)

**❌ Slop**
> Our candidate correlation engine leverages your existing ATS data to surface high-intent passive talent via automated multi-signal matching.

**Why it's slop:** "candidate correlation engine," "high-intent passive talent," "multi-signal matching" — every phrase is homework. The reader has to *decode* before they can care, and decoding is an exit ramp. This is how a recruiter would describe it to an investor, not to a mate.

**✅ Human**
> It takes your most placeable candidates and matches them to live open roles that fit, quietly, every day — so your best candidates are doing BD for you instead of sitting in the ATS.

**Why it works:** it's how you'd explain it to a stranger at a party. Plain words, one idea, and it names the thing they actually feel — good candidates going stale in the database. **Rules:** D3, B2 (one idea), anti-slop L2.

## 4. Generic personalisation vs the specificity ladder (D4)

**❌ Slop**
> I noticed your company is growing and thought you might be interested in what we do. I've been following your work and love what you're doing.

**Why it's slop:** "noticed you're growing," "been following your work," "love what you're doing" — merge-field flattery that could be sent to 10,000 people. It fakes research without doing any (that's the tell). Empty specificity reads as *more* automated, not less.

**✅ Human** *(climbing the ladder)*
> - **L1:** For {{niche}}, is the harder part surfacing the right candidates or getting the passive ones to engage?
> - **L2:** Saw {{company}} has three {{role}} searches live this month — that's a heavy load for a team your size.
> - **L3:** Your post on why contingent is dying for exec search stuck with me — you're basically describing the retained model we help desks move to.

**Why it works:** each rung could only have been sent to fewer people than the one above. L3 could not have been sent to anyone else — which is the whole game. **Never fake L3**; a wrong detail is worse than none. If you can't get past L1, open on the situation instead. **Rules:** D4, B3 (one concrete signal), B1.

## 5. Apologetic follow-up vs problem-led (C5)

**❌ Slop**
> Hey {{first}}, just following up on my last email — no worries at all if you're not interested! Totally understand you're busy. No pressure either way, and no need to hop on a call. Just let me know either way!

**Why it's slop:** it apologises for existing. "No worries if not," "no pressure either way," "no need to hop on a call," "just let me know" — every line shrinks the offer and signals we don't believe in it. A follow-up that talks itself out of the reply.

**✅ Human**
> Hey {{first}},
>
> The back-office admin is what eats a recruiter's week, and most of it doesn't need a human on it.
>
> Want a 2-min recording of it sourcing and drafting outreach on a live role? You'll see exactly where it buys the time back.

**Why it works:** opens on the *problem*, not an apology. The ask is a concrete give (a 2-min recording) answerable yes/no in three seconds. No self-shrinking close. **Rules:** C5 / [[feedback_non_apologetic_followups]], B7 (CTC not CTA), B8 (low-friction ask).

---

## 6. Forced proof vs real proof only (B10)

**❌ Slop**
> We've helped hundreds of agencies 10x their pipeline and generate millions in placements with our proven system.

**Why it's slop:** "hundreds," "10x," "millions," "proven system" — round, unverifiable, and it reads as marketing precisely because no real number is round and clean like that. It also breaks [[feedback_no_revenue_promises]] (we never promise revenue). A vague big claim lowers trust; it doesn't raise it.

**✅ Human**
> One of our recruiters, 63 and not technical, runs most of his desk through this now.

**Why it works:** a specific, real, human detail beats a big number. It's a *scene*, and it quietly handles the "this is too technical for me" objection. Where we do use figures, only the verified ones: 50+ OutboundOS deployments · 200+ GTME placements (98% still in role at 18 months) · 650+ agencies audited — never the pending ones. **Rules:** B10 (relevant proof), anti-slop L3, [[feedback_preflight_check]], [[feedback_no_revenue_promises]].

## 7. Subject lines — pitch vs internal-tone (C3)

**❌ Slop:** `Transform Your Recruitment Process with AI! 🚀` · `Quick question, {{first}}?` · `Following up`

**✅ Human:** `your back office` · `your best candidates` · `{{niche}} sourcing`

**Why:** the good ones read like an email from a colleague — 2–4 words, lowercase, no pitch, no emoji, no clickbait `?`. Title-case + exclamation + rocket emoji is the universal "this is a broadcast" signal. **Rules:** C3.

---

## The 60-second test (the shortcut that catches all of the above)
Read the message aloud. If it sounds like a person who has actually run a recruitment desk talking to another person, it passes. If it sounds like a press release or a landing page, it fails — go back to the 3 pre-writing questions (D1) and start from the situation, not the product.
