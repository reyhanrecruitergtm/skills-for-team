# Inspiration — Matt Gray, Bali Mastermind Founders Circle email

**Source:** Direct email from Matt Gray, May 2026
**Why saved:** Reference for Lisbon Mastermind marketing copy. Matt's structure, pacing, and tone are the gold standard for founder-event positioning.

---

## What to steal when writing Lisbon Mastermind copy

### 1. Opener that punches in
> "If you're a serious founder, the next 12 months will punish one weakness in your business."

Direct address, urgency, no preamble. Names a specific weakness immediately in the second line ("Distribution you don't control."). Use the same structure for Lisbon — name the weakness recruitment agencies will get punished for in the next 12 months.

### 2. Diagnose the reader's actual pain in short lines
Matt's middle section is a string of 1-2 line paragraphs naming the founder's exact internal experience:

> "Revenue is solid. But the margin tells a different story."
> "The business only works because you do."
> "Sales depend on you. Decisions bottleneck at you."
> "You're scaling effort, not systems."

Each line is a separate paragraph. White space carries weight. Reader nods along. The cumulative effect is "this guy gets me."

For Lisbon — write the recruitment agency version: "Placements are inconsistent. You can't predict next month's revenue. BD only happens when you have time. The team can't sell without you. The systems live in your head."

### 3. The contrarian truth pivot
> "AI will commoditize everything it can. Offers. Content. Services."
> "The one thing it cannot touch is you. Your story. Your voice. Your point of view."

The pivot moves from pain to mechanism. Names what's true now that the reader hasn't fully internalised. For Lisbon — pivot would be: "AI will commoditise sourcing, outreach, and screening. The thing it cannot touch is your taste, your relationships, your operational judgment. That's the moat you're building."

### 4. The frame upgrade
> "Your personal brand isn't a vanity play. It's a distribution engine."

He reframes a thing the reader already does (posting on LinkedIn) into something strategic (a distribution engine). For Lisbon — reframe Claude/AI from "fun toy" or "scary threat" into "the operating system every recruitment agency will run on in 18 months."

### 5. The event positioning — punchy, specific, scarce
> "20 founders. Zero noise."
> "One full day of working sessions, not lectures."
> "You bring your real bottlenecks. We work until there's a clear decision and a 30-day plan your team can run."

What works:
- **Number of attendees** stated upfront (creates scarcity)
- **"Not lectures"** — defines what it ISN'T as well as what it is
- **Outcome-driven** language ("clear decision", "30-day plan", "your team can run")
- **No fluff** about "amazing speakers" or "networking opportunities"

For Lisbon — likely "20 recruitment agency founders. One day of working sessions. You leave with your AI systems roadmap built, your first Claude Ops Manager spec'd, and a 30-day plan your team can execute."

### 6. The deliverables list (concrete, outcome-shaped)
> "You leave with your core constraint diagnosed. A personal brand distribution blueprint tied to revenue. A margin expansion plan. A delegation roadmap: what to cut, what to keep, what to install."
> "And 1–3 decisive moves already made. Plus a 30-day accountability session with me so the momentum doesn't stop."

Each deliverable is OUTCOME-shaped, not activity-shaped. Not "we'll workshop your strategy" but "you leave with your core constraint diagnosed."

For Lisbon, mirror this — every deliverable should answer "what will I LEAVE WITH that I don't have today?" not "what will we DO during the event?"

### 7. The price line — no apology, no tiers
> "20 seats. $5,000. One payment. No tiers. No upsells."

Direct, confident, removes friction. No comparison to "what others charge". No "early bird" gimmicks. For Lisbon, match the energy — single price, single payment, no upsells, named in one line.

### 8. The qualifier (who this is for)
> "If you're doing $30K–$500K+/month, you feel the market shifting, and you're ready to move, come join me and 19 other founders who are done waiting."

Three filters: revenue range, market awareness, readiness. Self-disqualifies tire-kickers. For Lisbon — revenue range for recruitment agencies could be "$25K-$100K+/month MRR or doing 5+ placements/quarter, you're feeling the AI disruption, and you're done waiting to install systems."

### 9. The P.S. (lightweight, honest)
> "P.S. Travel and accommodation not included. Spots are limited. Reply with any questions."

Three sentences. Sets expectations honestly (travel not included), creates scarcity (spots limited), opens door (reply with questions). For Lisbon, same energy.

## Voice rules Matt Gray follows (and Reyhan should too for Lisbon)

- One sentence per line, almost always
- Em dashes used sparingly (Matt uses them, but each one earns its place)
- No "transformative", "leverage", "unlock", "elevate"
- "Cannot" instead of "can't" for emphasis lines
- Italics for the internal monologue quote
- Direct second-person address throughout ("you", "your")
- Numbers, dates, prices stated bluntly without qualifying
- The reader is treated as competent, busy, and serious
