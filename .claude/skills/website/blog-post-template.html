What is candidate sourcing?

Candidate sourcing is the proactive process of identifying, researching, and engaging potential candidates who have not applied to your role. It sits at the very top of the recruiting funnel — before screening, before interviews — and it is the single biggest lever on how many quality conversations a desk can start.

Most agencies still source reactively: post a job, wait, and work whoever trickles in. The agencies that scale do the opposite. They build a repeatable engine that surfaces the right people the moment they're most likely to move.

In the last three years we've audited 650+ recruitment agencies, and the pattern is consistent: the ones stuck at founder-capacity are sourcing by hand; the ones scaling have turned it into a system. See our Sourcing Automation Playbook for the full build.

Rule of thumbIf a candidate could have been found by a saved search running automatically, a human should not be finding them manually.

Sourcing vs recruiting: what's the difference?

Sourcing finds and engages passive candidates; recruiting screens, qualifies, and places the ones who respond. They're different skills, different tools, and increasingly different roles on the desk.

The 5 candidate sourcing methods that work in 2026

The methods that still perform combine precise targeting with genuine personalisation. Here's what earns replies today — and what to stop doing.

  • Signal-based sourcing — trigger outreach on events (funding, job changes, hiring spikes) when candidates are most receptive.
  • Multi-source enrichment — combine LinkedIn, Apollo, and Apify so one profile becomes a full, contactable record.
  • Per-person AI outreach — Claude drafts messages off each person's real context, not a merge field.
  • Spray-and-pray InMail — the same template to 500 people. Reply rates now sit near zero.
  • Buying stale lists — unverified data burns your domain and your reputation.
The best sourcers in 2026 aren't the ones who contact the most people. They contact the right person, at the right moment, with the right message.

The modern candidate sourcing stack

A modern sourcing stack has four layers: find, enrich, personalise, and send. Each layer is now AI-assisted, which is what collapses hours of manual work into minutes.

Independent data backs the shift: 88% of organisations already use AI somewhere in hiring (Gartner, 2025), yet most agencies haven't connected the pieces into one flow. That gap is the opportunity. We break down each tool in The Recruiter's Tech Stack 2026.

  1. Find — Boolean and saved searches (see our Boolean search guide for recruiters).
  2. Enrich — Apollo primary, Apify as the verification backup.
  3. Personalise — a Claude AI Ops Manager drafting per-person openers.
  4. Send — Instantly (email) plus HeyReach (LinkedIn), sequenced.

Common candidate sourcing mistakes

Most sourcing fails for the same three reasons: no targeting, no personalisation, and no system. Fix these and reply rates climb without adding headcount.

The most expensive one is treating sourcing as a person instead of a process. When it lives only in the founder's head, it stops the day they get busy — which is every day. Document the workflow once and the whole desk can run it.

Frequently asked questions

What is candidate sourcing in simple terms?

It's proactively finding and contacting potential candidates who haven't applied yet — the top of the recruiting funnel, before any screening happens.

How is sourcing different from recruiting?

Sourcing starts conversations with passive candidates; recruiting screens and places the ones who respond. Different skills, tools, and success metrics.

What tools do I need to automate candidate sourcing?

A find layer (Boolean/saved search), enrichment (Apollo + Apify), an AI layer for personalised outreach (Claude), and a sender (Instantly + HeyReach).

How many candidate conversations should a sourcing system generate?

A well-built agency sourcing engine targets 30-50 qualified candidate conversations per month, per pipeline.
