I moved away from OpenAI.

Then I read what Anthropic's CEO actually said on record.

Now I don't know what to believe.

Here's the full picture — both sides.

—

OpenAI made their move first.

→ January 2024 — rewrote their usage policy and removed the explicit ban on military and warfare use

→ February 2026 — signed a $200M Pentagon contract covering, in their own words, "warfighting and enterprise domains"

→ Sam Altman later admitted the timing "looked opportunistic and sloppy"

No ambiguity. They chose the contract.

—

Then everyone started calling Anthropic the saint.

And on the surface, it looks that way.

→ The Pentagon blacklisted Anthropic in February 2026 — the first American company to receive that designation

→ Anthropic refused to accept "any lawful use" language for their models

→ They sued the Pentagon on March 9th, claiming First Amendment retaliation

That's a real stand. I won't pretend otherwise.

—

But here's what Dario Amodei actually said. Verbatim.

• "Anthropic isn't categorically opposed to autonomous weapons."

• "The reliability is not there yet."

• "We have offered to work with the Department of War on R&D to improve the reliability of these systems."

Their objection isn't ethical.

It's a product readiness issue.

The moment they believe Claude is capable enough for battlefield use — they've already told you what they'll do.

—

And the ownership structure makes it worse.

→ Google invested $3B in Anthropic — 14% stake

→ Google built Project Maven — AI specifically designed for drone targeting

→ Amazon invested $8B in Anthropic

→ Amazon runs the $9B JWCC Pentagon cloud contract — the infrastructure US military AI already sits on

Anthropic fights the Pentagon in court.

Their two biggest investors are already embedded in the same machine.

—

Anthropic was blacklisted on February 27th.

Claude was used to generate 1,000 airstrike targets against Iran within 72 hours.

Via Palantir. Through Amazon's cloud. Indirectly funded by Google.

—

Both companies will likely end up in the same place — just on different timelines.

But different timelines still matters.

I do believe Anthropic is the better choice right now — not because they're morally pure, but because they're actively resisting where OpenAI is actively running toward.

Claude Code in particular is worth calling out.

While agentic AI products like Clawbot race to ship features, Claude Code is the only one I've used that actually pushes back when something doesn't feel right — asks permission before taking irreversible actions, flags risk before executing, stays honest about what it doesn't know.

That's not a feature. That's the product reflecting the company's current values.

"Not ready yet" isn't the same as "never."

But it does buy time. And right now, time is what the rest of us need to figure out what we actually want from these companies.
