### Clay vs Pin.com
Clay vs. Pin.com: Who wins the sourcing system race?

I put them head-to-head on a live search: an Advanced Planning Attorney in Atlanta.

The results were a wake-up call for anyone still trying to force one tool to do everything.

The Pin.com Experience (8 minutes):
• 257 candidates found, all of them met at least 75% requirements.
• Search Engine and UI is built for recruiters.
• High relevance: it actually "understood" the LLM in Taxation requirement.
• One-click enrollment into LinkedIn and Email outreach.
• Pulls candidates data from 20+ data sources.

The Clay Experience (12+ minutes):
• 82 candidates were found.
• UI is hard to navigate for someone who isn't tech savvy.
• Requires building a grading prompt and logic for each role.
• Can be decent if you're sourcing for tech roles or similar but fails on niche searches.
• Still the GTM powerhouse, but felt slow for niche sourcing.
• Relies on LinkedIn as the main source of candidates.

My Verdict?

Clay is a world-class GTM engine. If you are building intent-driven outbound, it's a non-negotiable. But for high-speed, high-accuracy candidate sourcing? Pin.com is currently eating its lunch.

In RecruiterGTM, we don't teach you to fall in love with one tool. We teach you to build an engine where every part has a specific job.

Also, you don't need a human to find the talent anymore. You need a system to find them and an operator (a GTM Engineer or Jr. Recruiter) to engage them.

### German client OutboundOS case study
Case Study of the week: Outbound when your ICP is NOT on LinkedIn

We are currently implementing OutboundOS (our intent driven multichannel outbound engine) for a German client who recruits in a very niche corner of property management.

Here was the situation:

Here is the stack we used:

2️⃣ Clean the list before you contact anyone

Now the client has:
✔️ One Clay workbook that holds their whole outbound engine
✔️ Fresh company and role data that does not rely on LinkedIn at all

### AI adoption stats post
"Everyone is using AI now."

That is not even close to true.

This visualization (from Steven Bartlett) made it painfully clear:

84% of humanity (6.8B people) have never used AI. Not once.
16% (1.3B people) have only used free chatbots.
Only 15–25M people (~0.3%) actually pay for AI tools.
Only 2–5M people (~0.04%) use AI for coding and building real systems.

Recruitment is even further behind.

Less than 5% of recruitment businesses are using AI systems for core operations right now. Using ChatGPT to write LinkedIn posts does not count lol.

### 1-10 Hormozi close
The simplest close I use right now came from a Hormozi video. And it actually works.

On every strategy call, I ask a version of the 1–10 question:
"From 1 to 10, how confident are you that this is the right move for your business?"

Most people say 7.

👉 First question: "Why is it not a 0?"
They then start telling me all the reasons it makes sense. They talk themselves into the value. I just listen.

👉 Second question: "What would make it a 10?"
Most of them pause. Because for my offer, I am already giving them everything. There is not a magic extra thing. The value is already there.

I am not trying to "convince" anyone. I just help them verbalise what they already know.

## Tone calibration notes
- Comfortable referencing own failures and early-stage uncertainty: "I'm only in month 2..."
- Self-deprecating when appropriate, never when talking about the product
- Mixes tactical (Clay waterfalls, specific playbook names) with philosophical (Covey, Drucker, Hormozi)
- "lol" used once -- signals he's not robotic, but use very sparingly
- Rhetorical questions used to transition: "So who teaches them all that?", "Why are you still only hiring locally?"
